How we review RegulateED together.

This site is where we shape RegulateED before anyone builds it: the product workflow, the research behind it, and every comment we make about it. This page explains how to use it, and how your comments turn into the next sprint.

Where this is going

When the review sprints are done, everything we agree becomes a pitch presentation for RegulateED, with prototype screens and a demo running on made-up data. So every comment, decision and research answer here is building toward the pitch.

1How a sprint works

A sprint is one collaboration session. We go right through the site together: commenting, asking questions, drawing and discussing.

  1. 1. CollaborateAnyone starts a thread anywhere, at any time, and attaches it to a category. The person who starts a thread owns it.
  2. 2. DiscussThe discussion stays inside the thread. Iris answers questions there, and brings research back there.
  3. 3. End each threadEvery thread ends one of two ways: Resolved (we agreed something changes) or No action (discussed, nothing changes).
  4. 4. Close the sprintAdam closes the sprint. Each category now holds its actions, findings and agreements.
  5. 5. UpdateThose category summaries are Iris's update list. She builds them into the site, and the next sprint opens fresh.
Where we are now

Sprint 2 is open. Every thread that was still open from Sprint 1 has moved into Sprint 2 and is attached to a category. Sprint 1 is history: you can still read it (open Sprint 2, then Sprint 1 history), but it is out of the way.

Want to practise first?

The demo page is a safe place to try comments, drawings, links and chats. Nothing there is part of the product, and it is wiped after testing.

2Getting in, and your name

  • Open regulateed.com/review and enter the review password. The device remembers it.
  • The first time you comment, you type your name. Type it the same way every time (for example jordan), because it is how the site knows which replies are new for you. To change it, open Comments and tap change next to your name.
  • Lock forgets the password on that device. It asks first.

3Finding your way

  • The home page is the workflow, in four steps: Data collection, Insights analysis, Activity execution, and Trends and effectiveness. Under them sit the Foundations: the event record, privacy, evidence, frameworks, sprints and research.
  • Tap a step to go into it. The trail at the top shows where you are; tap any part of it to go back. Up one level sits at the bottom of every page.
  • The buttons along the bottom are the review tools: Sprint 2 (its categories), Add comment, Draw, Comments, Iris and Lock.

4Leaving a comment or a drawing

A comment
Tap Add comment, tap the spot on the page you mean, choose its category, type, then tap Save. A numbered pin marks the spot. A thread cannot be saved without a category; if you are not sure, choose Not sure: Iris files it.
A drawing
Tap Draw, circle or scribble on the screen, then tap Done. A picture of the screen with your drawing goes into a new comment.
A reply
Tap a pin to open its thread, then reply underneath.
Tips that help Iris file it right
  • One point per comment. Three ideas make three comments.
  • Put it on the spot it is about. A comment on Grade 3 belongs on the Grade 3 page.
  • If you want evidence, tick This is a research request (or ask in the thread). The thread shows Research requested, then Research in progress, and the answer comes back in the same thread with a link to its research page.

5Knowing when something is back

  • The message card. When Iris has written to you, a card saying "Jordan, message from IRIS" greets you as you arrive. Tap Open Iris Chat to read and answer, or Got it.
  • A red dot on a pin means a thread you started or joined has a reply you have not read.
  • The Comments button shows how many are new, and the list puts New for you at the top.
  • The Iris button has a red dot when there is a new message in Iris Chat.

Iris checks for new comments and messages every 10 minutes while a work session is open, and otherwise at the start of the next one. Answers are quick, but not instant.

6What a thread's state means

Every thread shows its category and its state, at the top of the thread and in its category.

You seeIt meansWhat to do
OpenBeing discussed.Reply in the thread.
Iris asked youIris needs one answer from you.Reply in the thread.
Research requestedIris is finding the evidence.Nothing: the answer comes back in the thread.
ResolvedWe agreed something changes. The last reply says what.Nothing. It rolls up into its category.
No actionDiscussed, and nothing changes.Nothing.

The owner of a thread, the person who started it, ends it with Resolved or No action at the bottom of the thread. Iris can end it too, with a reply saying what happened.

7Categories

Tap Sprint 2 on the bottom bar. You see the sprint's categories, each with how many threads are open, being researched, resolved and closed with no action. Tap a category to open it:

Actions
What will change, gathered as threads are resolved.
Findings
What we learned, from the threads and the research, each with its research link.
Agreements
What we agreed as threads closed.
Research
The research answers behind the category.
Threads
Every thread in the category: its owner, its state, and a link that opens it where it was made.

The categories in Sprint 2 are Check-in design, Learning and activities, Devices and access, Pilot and data, Privacy and portals, and General. When the sprint closes, each category's actions, findings and agreements are the update list for the next sprint.

In Iris Chat, tap Link a spot, then tap anywhere on the page. A spot chip goes into your message, so you can point at something on one page while talking about another.

9Iris Chat

Tap Iris on the bottom bar, or the Iris Chat tab in Comments. This is your own conversation with Iris, for anything that is not about one spot on the page:

  • questions about the plan, the sprints or how to use the site
  • asking for research
  • checking that Iris understood you

Iris Chat is general chat, outside any thread, and is not filed in a category. Adam can read it too.

10Research for the team

On the workflow, open Foundations › Research for the team. Every research question asked in review gets a page there, always in the same shape:

  • the question, in the words of the person who asked
  • the short answer
  • how strong the evidence is: Strong, Moderate, Emerging, or Weak or contested
  • what the evidence shows
  • what it means for RegulateED
  • the sources

Following Jordan's rule, sources are scholarly and from the last 10 years first. An older study appears only when it is the founding work, and it is marked as older. A question still being researched shows as Being researched until its answer is in.

11Earlier sprints and older pins

  • The pages as they stood in each sprint are kept at their own address, so a comment can always be seen exactly where it was made. Foundations › Sprints and comments lists them.
  • Comments from an earlier sprint show as outlined pins tagged with their sprint, such as S1. A dashed pin means the exact spot changed, so the pin sits on the nearest part that survived.
  • Nothing is ever deleted.

12The maintenance pause

Before a new sprint opens, the site goes into a short maintenance pause. A note says so at the top, and Add comment, Draw and replies are hidden. You can still read everything. Nothing you wrote is lost, and comments open again when the new sprint is up.

13What never goes in a comment

  • No real child's name, photo or details, and no family's.
  • No school records, health information or anything from a real classroom or pilot.
  • Made-up examples are fine, for example "a Grade 3 class after recess".
