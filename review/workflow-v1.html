Insights analysis

Events become insights when they are compared: this moment against the class's usual, the same moment across the week, and weeks against weeks.

Three kinds of insight

Now

This event against this class's usual for the same moment: "Back from recess, 10 of 21 unsettled; usually 6."

Pattern

The same moment across the week: Recess is unsettled four days out of five. This is the insight that opens Activity execution.

Trend

Weeks against weeks, with and without an activity: is the recess pattern easing since wall pushes started? Step 4 measures it.

What the evidence says, in one line

Movement breaks and slow breathing have the best evidence; heavy work, Brain Gym style moves and weighted vests the weakest. Almost nothing has been tested on a class that is already Red, within minutes: RegulateED's own re-checks could be the first.

Two kinds of evidence

The research grade says what is known in general. The class's own record says what works here. When they disagree, as wall pushes do above, both are shown: the class record ranks the options, the research grade stays visible.

Who sees it
  • Teacher: the ranked options for each moment
  • School OT: which activities work, by grade and moment
  • Principal: whether classes need fewer resets over a term
To decide

Foundations

The event record: what every check-in, activity and re-check stores.

An event has to know whether an activity followed it and what shift was expected, or no trend can measure the activity. That is the one change to the data from today's design.

Adam, review comment, 18 November"The data collection events will need to know if they have activity expectations as part of a trend monitoring element, so the data needs to be considered in this way."

Event (check-in)

  • Moment, date and time
  • Teacher, class, grade
  • Method (1 to 5)
  • Children present
  • Counts: Green, Yellow, Red, or the class color
  • Purpose: check-in

Activity run

  • Responds to: the event
  • Activity and family
  • Led by: teacher, or the app per child (G4, G5)
  • Started, length

Expectation

  • Expected shift, e.g. Red to 1 or fewer
  • Re-check due, e.g. 5 minutes
  • Set from the activity's defaults

Event (re-check)

  • Same fields as a check-in
  • Purpose: re-check
  • Linked to the activity run

Outcome

  • Actual shift: re-check minus check-in
  • Met or not met
  • Feeds effectiveness by class, moment, activity

Fields in coral are new: they exist so trends can measure activities.

The baseline

Check-ins with no activity run are kept just the same. They are the "usual" an insight compares against, and the weeks without an activity a trend compares with.

Still nothing about a child

Every record is about the class and the moment. G4 and G5 answers arrive unnamed; whether they carry an anonymised token is still to decide.

Foundations

How we grade the evidence for each activity.

Strong

Several good trials or a meta-analysis in children, consistent on outcomes that matter in class: behavior, attention, measured stress.

Moderate

Good evidence, but mostly in adults, in small trials, or mixed in children. Reasonable to use; worth measuring.

Emerging

Promising early studies, a plausible mechanism or a strong practice tradition, not yet tested well in classrooms.

Weak or contested

Reviews find little or no effect, or its claims are disputed. Use with care, never as a promise, and say so.

Why it matters

A health product for children must not claim more than the science supports. The full review with every source is in the repo: 2026-11-18 RegulateED activity evidence review.

Step 1

Data collection

Every child is asked "How are you feeling?" the way their age can answer, and every answer belongs to an event.

Every child is asked "How are you feeling?" and answers Green, Yellow or Red. How they answer changes as they grow. This matrix shows which method each grade uses.

Branches

Method 1

Teacher-led group question

The teacher asks the whole class and reads the room, then records one color for the class.

  1. The teacher opens the event: Recess, today, their class.
  2. Asks the class, "How are we feeling?"
  3. Children answer out loud, hold up a color card or point.
  4. The teacher picks one color for the class. Saved.
Why K and G1

No reading and no device in a child's hand. It is what a K or G1 teacher already does, and it takes under a minute.

What is recorded

One color for the class, saved to the event. Nothing about any child.

Watch for

Loud voices sway it and children copy each other, so a quiet child in Red can go unseen. That is why the entry survey runs alongside it in the same grades.

Method 2

Event-based entry survey

The teacher's iPad stands at the classroom door. As the children come in, each one taps the face that matches how they feel.

  1. The teacher opens the event and sets their iPad on a stand at the door.
  2. As the line comes in: "How are you feeling?"
  3. Each child taps one face as they pass. A soft chime says "got it".
  4. The teacher closes the event and sees the count of each color.
Why K to G3

Faces, not words, so no reading is needed, and every child counts the same. It stops after G3: older children are seen tapping by the line, and answer more honestly in private.

What is recorded

A count of each color and the total taps against the children present. Never a name, and never the time or order of a tap, so a tap cannot be matched to the child who walked in.

Watch for, and the guard
  • Double taps: a short pause after each tap
  • Pressing for fun: taps checked against children present
  • A bad round: the teacher can discard the event

Method 3

QR code survey: anonymous, by event

The event's QR code goes up on the board. Children scan it on a classroom iPad or Chromebook and answer a short web form. No name, no login, no app.

  1. The teacher opens the event; RegulateED shows its QR code.
  2. Children scan it, or type the short link.
  3. Pick a color, then "Where do you feel it?"
  4. Send. The answer joins the event.
Why G2 to G4

Children now read simple words and can use a shared device, so each answers alone and adds one more thing: where they feel it. It stops after G4, when the app does everything the form does plus the reset.

What is recorded

One unnamed answer per child, pooled into the event: the color and the body spot. The QR code carries the event, never the child.

Method 4

RegulateED mobile app

The full RegulateED loop on the child's own school iPad: check in, a 2-minute reset chosen for how they feel, a re-check, and a Focus Token when they reach Green.

Why G4 and G5

Children read well and use their school device on their own, so they can run a reset themselves without the teacher stopping the class.

What is recorded

The check-in, body spot, the reset done and the re-check, each sent from the child's own iPad to the event. So the school also sees whether resets work.

The concept, G4 and G5

Every child sends their own result, from their own school iPad.

From G4 the RegulateED app is on each child's school iPad. When the teacher opens an event, every iPad in the room asks the question at once. Each child answers privately and their result goes back to the event.

  1. The teacher opens the event from their own screen: After lunch, today, their class.
  2. Every child's iPad shows the check-in.
  3. Each child answers in private, does their reset, re-checks.
  4. Each result is sent to the event, with no name attached.
  5. The teacher sees the class split fill in, live.
Why it matters

Every child is heard, nobody sees anybody else's answer, and the teacher does not have to stop the lesson to collect it.

Privacy

What travels is the answer and the event: color, body spot, the reset and the re-check. Never a name, photo or voice. It is designed to stay inside student privacy law (FERPA for school records, COPPA for children's apps).

To decide

Whether results stay fully anonymous, or carry an anonymised school token (no name) so a school OT can follow one child's IEP or 504 goals over 30, 60 and 90 days, as the framework plans.

From results to action

Every result becomes an insight, and every insight offers a way to shift the class.

The check-in tells the teacher where the class is. Insights turn that into what to do next: activities designed to move a mostly Red room toward Yellow and Green, offered as options the teacher chooses and applies to the class.

1Check inAny method, any grade
2InsightWhat the split says
3OptionsActivities matched to it
4ApplyThe teacher picks one
5Re-checkDid the class shift?
The insight

Read from the split, never the average alone: how much of the room is Red, Yellow or Green, at which moment, compared with this class's usual. For example, "Back from recess, almost half the class is unsettled: more than usual for them."

The options

Two or three activities designed for that result, each about 2 minutes and done at the desk or as a class. A mostly Red room gets calming heavy work and slow breathing; a mostly Yellow room gets soothing touch or breathing. The teacher chooses; RegulateED never runs one on its own.

The proof

A quick re-check after the activity is a second event linked to the first, so the shift is measured: Red to Yellow, Yellow to Green. Over weeks, insights learn which activities work best for this class at this moment, and offer those first.

By grade

K to G3: the teacher leads the activity with the whole class. G4 and G5: the teacher can apply it to the class, and the app can also offer each child a reset of their own.

What insights learn over time

When

The moments a class usually struggles, such as after recess or before lunch, so a reset can be planned before the room tips into Red.

What works

Which activities shift this class, at which moment, ranked by the re-checks. The best one is offered first.

Progress

Whether the class needs fewer resets over the weeks: the trend a teacher, a school OT or a principal wants to see.

To develop

The RegulateED activity library: each activity designed by an OT for a result, an age band and a place (at the desk, standing, whole class). The examples above come from the framework's four groups: heavy work, movement across the body, touch resets and rhythmic breathing.

To decide

Green, Yellow and Red cannot show a sluggish, low-energy class, the one that needs waking up, not calming down. The framework used a fourth state, Blue, for that. Does the check-in need a way to tell it apart?

Privacy by design

No record about a named child, in any grade.

What we keep
  • The event: moment, date, time, teacher, class
  • Which method collected it
  • The class color, the counts, or each unnamed answer
  • From G4: the reset done and the re-check
What we never keep
  • Names, logins with names, or class rosters
  • Photos, video or voice
  • The time or order of a tap at the door
  • Anything a classmate could see on another child's screen
K to G3, methods 1 and 2

No child is ever recorded: the class color or the counts only.

G2 to G4, method 3

Unnamed answers. The QR code carries the event, not the child.

G4 and G5, methods 4 and 5

Sent from each child's own iPad, no name. An anonymised token for IEP and 504 tracking is still to decide.
